## Confirmed single-fault defect and correction

Gray pp.33–34 requires redundancy in **procedures**, as well as stored bits.
The inherited engines repaired the tracks, executed each primary operation using
that primary cell's raw Address/Age, then copied the result to all R holders.
Consequently one bad clock could skip an operation in **every** copy. Increasing
R from 3 to 5 did not help. A deterministic counterexample schedules `HOLD:=1`
at address 20 and age 10, then changes only that cell's age to 11: the old rule
leaves repaired HOLD[20]=0, for both R=3 and R=5.

The correction executes each holder's copy independently. Holder x, storing the
bit of y=x+o, uses Age(x), (Address(x)+o) mod Q, and its own interpreter registers.
The output rule is

\[
 T'_{x,r}=F_{Age(x)}(\operatorname{Maj}_R T,
             y=x+r-h,\ (Address(x)+r-h)\bmod Q),\qquad h=(R-1)/2.
\]

Holder-side mailbox/address-change wipes still follow this computation. The
dependency radius for ordinary track ops is D+2h: R=3,D=3 and R=5,D=1 both
remain range 5. This agrees with the holder control convention already assumed
by the simulated-layer interpreter. It is a defensible completion of Gray's
implicit redundant computation, not a claim of a complete fault-tolerance proof.

`tests/test_fault_containment.py`: 18 adversarial cases (3 fault types, 3
boundary/interior positions, 2 redundancy settings), each trying all holders;
2 CUDA/NumPy comparisons; 2 complete work periods with a clock fault exactly
at the final Info:=Hold update. **22 tests passed**. The latter verifies actual
decoded Address/Age/Flags and would retain simulated Age=0 under the old rule.
Both full-period tests were also run against the saved legacy CUDA binary:
**both failed**, each with one incorrect decoded Info bit. Thus the tests
distinguish the old and corrected physical rules, not just two reference models.

## Damaged-state hierarchy counterexample and correction

The next test found a second defect: in a 64-cell simulated ring just before
trickle-down, set Info[61]=1 and damage only cell 63's Address and Age to zero.
The direct upper rule repairs that cell to Address=63, Age=3422, WF1=1.
The interpreted rule previously decoded the same Address/Age but **WF1=0**,
because its IWF shortcut used old registers instead of the computed fields.

`compile_workspace_flags` now computes the timing predicate and signal-site
selection bit-serially from HOLD.Address/HOLD.Age, majority-repairs the
appropriate gathered Info copies, and combines the result with computed Flag1.
It uses ordinary microprogram primitives, preserving locality and inspectability.
The lower schedule grows from 10894 to 11418 steps, still within U₀=16384.
`tests/test_tower_faults.py` checks both WF1 and WF2, **every encoded field and
track copy**, over two simulated steps starting with damaged Address/Age;
both cases pass. The third test now rejects nonlocal R=5,D=3; R=5,D=1 is supported.

## Computed SimBit: a distinct locality ambiguity (D10)

Gray [p.41](../../../papers_txt/gray_readers_guide.txt) asks for a neighbour's computed
SimBit. Interpreting this as the final primary after the p.33 wipe is not
automatically a range-5 rule. A distinguishing test sets x=Q−1 and y=x−2=Q−3,
starts just before the trickle window, and puts 1 in all Info bits. The three
cells x−7,x−6,x−5 have addresses shifted by 7; Flag1=1 at y,y+1,x. Correcting
only x−7 changes y's left majority, toggling its address-change wipe and
Info′(y) from 0 to 1. The computed Address/Age at x are unchanged, so the
literal post-wipe reading would toggle WF1′(x) using information seven sites
away. Inputs in x's radius-5 neighborhood are identical.

`tests/test_workspace_timing.py` reproduces this at Q=32 and at Gray's Q=8192,
with U=128Q and R=5. This differs from Masumori's invalid accessibility example:
here the address guard **is** satisfied. Current repaired Info remains a
provisional local interpretation; another possible resolution is to define a
pre-wipe signal substep explicitly. A full source-faithful timing convention
and its fault-containment proof remain research work.
