# Second independent audit: G15 and the three-level hybrid

2026-10-01. I reviewed changes since `7caedb2`, Gray §5.1 and pp. 33–36, the updated report, source, tests, and on-disk receipts. I rebuilt G15, ran the 44-test suite, made new classifier and receipt checks, ran three successive G15 physical macrosteps, and continued two faulty physical runs past their reported hand-off. I changed only files in the round-two audit namespaces. GPU runs began after `nvidia-smi`, `uptime`, and `ps` checks; no other GPU job was running then. Host memory stayed well below 40 GiB.

## Verdict

**G15 remains a genuine fixed radius-5, 260-bit rule with its own compiled instruction table.** A fresh build matches the cache and manifest (Q=512, U=112,608, QU=57,655,296, 17,100 gates, 329 occupied passes). My two nonzero rings closed at each of three successive physical periods. The phase receipt crosses a level-2 commit and uses an independent NumPy transition for the level-2 reference. These are strong finite self-simulation checks, not an all-state proof.

**The error runs give qualified empirical support for Gray-like amplification, but the formal error and Proposition 4 claims remain overstated.** Standalone dense 100×100 and selected sparse errors, one/two-colony wipes, and the 61 selected hybrid cases have the reported sampled outcomes. The combined E0 campaigns certify the burst without the surrounding E0 errors; their first scenario actually fails Gray's level-1 isolation condition in the full error set. G15 fails the literal all-field spatial clause of Proposition 4, as the report's own strict verdict records. The 61-case level-2 result continues on the level-1 automaton after a short physical slice; it is not 61 fully physical level-2 repairs. Two new physical continuations support the hand-off in those cases, but do not prove it for arbitrary residual state. No noise threshold or full physical level-2 macrostep has been established.

## Findings, by severity

### 1. High — the updated summary's all-campaign amplification claim is contradicted by its new receipts

**Where:** `Report/fixed_rule/design_optimization/REPORT.md:97-112,117-123`; counterexamples in `figs/fixed_rule/design_optimization/level1_campaign/G15_b92_full_wipe2.json`, `G15_b93_full_wipe3.json`, `G15_b95_full_box1000.json`, and `G15_b96_full_wipe2_e0all.json`.

The summary says that across **all** campaign receipts no undamaged upper colony is ever wrong, every error looks like a one-time one/two-cell level-0 error upstairs, and 1,755 of 2,324 rings never change the upper state while the other 569 do so at exactly one time. The newer large-error receipts explicitly do the opposite. For b93 gather1/mid, the physical wipe touches colonies 255–257, but after the first upper step decoded cells 252–260 are wrong; the wrong interval persists and grows over all four recorded steps. There are 25 distinct large-error scenarios with wrong decoded cells at multiple upper times (five straddling b92, ten b93, five b95, five b96). This is expected behavior for errors assigned to level 2, and §26 describes it correctly. The unrestricted summary is stale.

**Reproduction:** the command that produced `larger_burst_counterexample.log` is in the command list below; `count_receipts.log` shows current raw receipts total 2,441 error rings/9,474 recorded upper steps, and a scenario-key dedup gives 2,415/9,376, with 25 multi-time wrong-state cases. These two totals have different duplicate policies, so neither is claimed as the uniquely correct publication total. Both show why 2,324/9,053 needs an explicit receipt inclusion list. **Invalidates:** the updated summary's universal scope and its aggregate counts, not the narrower isolated-error results. **Fix:** scope the one-time/undamaged-colony statement to the eligible isolated level-1 and ≤2-colony cases; publish a receipt manifest and recompute counts after each new batch.

### 2. High — Gray classifier gives wrong answers, and combined-noise “certificates” omit the full error set

**Where:** `gacsca/fixed_rule/design_optimization/gray_errors.py:75-90,139-151`; `experiments/fixed_rule/design_optimization/level1_campaign.py:281-317`; `REPORT.md:84-88,1909-1925`.

Gray requires a candidate level-1 set **S ⊆ E minus E0** and isolation from **E minus (S∪E0)**. `classify` silently removes E0 points from S and can return `level1=True` for the original S. For example, a dense 3×3 cluster plus isolated `(1000,0)` returns true although that singleton is E0 and belongs to S. For large E, `_level0_dense` detects isolated singletons but misses isolated adjacent *pairs*: a dense 36×36 cluster is level 1 with an isolated pair at `(1000,0),(1001,0)`, yet the shortcut treats the pair as non-E0 and returns false for the cluster. These are counterexamples to the classifier's advertised exactness.

For b81/b82 the driver calls `classify(box, Q, U)` rather than `classify(box, full_E, Q, U)`. Reproducing the CUDA E0-grid hash for the first receipt's seed 57 finds hits at `(131259,1524)`, `(131269,1558)`, `(131259,1618)`, and `(131369,1655)` outside, but within 24 in both coordinates of, the dense box. Those hits are not E0 in the combined set. Even adding only these four points to E changes the box's level-1 verdict from true to false by Gray condition (iv). The code's receipt says true.

**Reproduction:** `check_classes.py` → `check_classes.log`, and `classifier_membership.log`; both print the input and opposite verdicts. **Invalidates:** the formal “certified level-1 error plus E0 noise” description of b81/b82 and any claim that the classifier handles arbitrary finite E. Their measured recovery remains a valid combined-noise experiment. The 548 standalone G15 and 128 G14 certificate counts are not refuted by this particular full-E problem, although their sparse cases were not independently exhaustively reclassified. **Fix:** reject S intersecting E0, detect isolated adjacent pairs in the dense shortcut, and classify each scenario against its realized complete error set (or label it an isolated burst under added E0 noise without a formal certificate).

### 3. Medium — sampled and majority-based Proposition 4 verdicts are weaker than the stated proposition

**Where:** `level1_campaign.py:139-153,332-392,455-475`; `rule_g.py:484-493`; `REPORT.md:1634-1798,1985-2003`; Gray reader's guide p. 35–36 (`papers_txt/gray_readers_guide.txt:1590-1630`).

`prop4_check` correctly enumerates candidate aligned two-colony/two-period boxes **for the events it receives**. Events are sampled every Q=512 ticks. A physical difference outside the box lasting between samples is invisible; `prop4_limits.log` shows the checker rejects a supplied out-of-box event and accepts the same event when omitted. In b81/b82, the driver also removes every site hit by noise in the immediately preceding tick before recording differences. A persistent difference at such a site is removed too. This is a limitation of the check, not evidence that a persistent difference actually occurred in these runs.

The newer `simbits` check votes five Info copies and restricts attention to represented logical positions. Gray calls the stored primary and secondary copies SimBit fields; voting is a decoder operation, not equality of those fields. In b91, all 10 cases pass represented-SimBit confinement but only 9 pass per-copy Info-slot confinement. For G15 b62, the strict all-field spatial result is 0/64. Gray's Proposition 4 explicitly says simulation-structure **fields** equal the deterministic system outside its boxes, while the preceding prose acknowledges that Mailbox and Workspace of undamaged colonies may temporarily receive wrong data. This tension in Gray's informal guide does not make G15's weaker split verdict the literal proposition, nor prove that Gray's full construction cannot satisfy it.

**Reproduction:** `prop4_limits.log`, `count_receipts.log`, and `G15_prop4_fields_box100_random.json`; `prop4_fields.py` recorded mail/history differences outside the two-colony box. **Invalidates:** “G15 meets Proposition 4” as a literal, continuous-time, all-field claim. It supports sampled full-state time recovery and sampled logical SimBit confinement. **Fix:** name those two empirical properties directly; use per-tick field checks for a literal test, keep per-copy results separate, and do not mask a fresh E0 hit when testing whether an older difference persists.

### 4. Medium — level-2 repair is a hybrid continuation, with a finite hand-off check

**Where:** `experiments/fixed_rule/design_optimization/three_level_g.py:300-354,416-459`; `REPORT.md:1895-1907,1979-1985,2050-2088`.

`repair2` physically simulates a 64–192-cell level-1 slice for four macrosteps, checks one-step decoded closure away from the slice ends, then applies **only the decoded difference** to a full level-1 ring and continues on the level-1 automaton. All 27 recorded hand-offs differ from their reference only in Info; in the 12 that record represented-slot attribution, none of the unexplained bits occupies such a slot (`count_receipts.log`). The residual-state XOR is often nonzero: the rich 64-colony wipe has 410 unexplained physical Info bits. The check that none occupies a represented upper-state slot does not itself prove that no discarded bit can affect future physical behavior: G15 does read unrepresented special Info at Addresses 3 and Q−3. A new five-copy perturbation at Q−3 changed five `wf1` sites on the next tick despite identical decoded states at injection, though in that probe it vanished by the period boundary (`special_info.log`).

This is an **evidence gap, not a found macrostep failure**. I continued two rich faulty physical slices for two further periods. Their residual 125 and 165 Info bits lay at Addresses 387–391 and 120–124/387–391; the first additional decoded step matched the upper automaton, the physical state then became identical to a canonical encoding of the same decoded state, and the second decoded step remained identical to the canonical continuation (`handoff_continuation.log`, `handoff_commit.log`). The original per-step closure checks establish closure for each observed faulty state *through* the hand-off, inside the checked interior. They do not establish closure for every possible future faulty physical state or for the full physical level-2 period.

`copy2` overwrites level-1 states directly. It is a possible adversarial upper-level error model, but no physical injection/run realizes those 17 copies. Likewise, b92/b93/b95/b96 were not continued as those **particular** physical errors to a level-2 boundary; §26.5 tested analogous wipes in a different healthy level-2 setup. **Invalidates:** wording that the entire physical→level 1→level 2 chain was directly observed in all 61 cases, or that §26.5 repairs those named b-batch errors. **Fix:** state the hybrid boundary plainly, save and continue representative full physical states after hand-off, and derive a proof or exhaustive condition showing that discarded residual slots cannot alter later decoded macrosteps. Keep direct upper-level copy experiments separate from physical injections.

### 5. Low — manifest pins the current artifact, not the current recipe

**Where:** `gacsca/fixed_rule/design_optimization/candidates.py:179-231`; `tests/fixed_rule/design_optimization/test_front_candidate.py:576-607`.

The new manifest catches a flipped ROM and every current cache matches a fresh build. But `load()` reconstructs parameters from the cache, verifies the cache against the manifest, and never compares either with `RECIPES[name]`. Monkeypatching only G15's recipe to set `stage_wipe=False` still loads the cached `stage_wipe=True` rule and reports success (`recipe_mutation.log`). **Invalidates:** the literal claim that loading detects a stale cache after a recipe change, not today's G15 artifact. **Fix:** pin a normalized recipe digest in the manifest and check it at load, or compare current recipe parameters with the cached parameters before trusting the manifest.

### 6. Low — G15's two wipe times are a documented adaptation, and §24 asserts a test control that is absent

**Where:** `rule_g.py:484-493`, `rule_g.py:440-471`; `tests/fixed_rule/design_optimization/test_front_candidate.py:529-573`; `REPORT.md:1473-1540,1735-1765`.

Gray describes wiping Mailbox and Workspace at **each stage start**. G15 clears Hold at E0−1 and histories/mail at commit, while scratch is cleared at commit by `clear_ws`. My one-tick probe confirms those exact clearings and that commit still copies Hold into Info (`stage_wipe.log`). The stage-age order leaves the early program's Hold stores after E0−1 (E0=16,416), the special courier at 23,904, and the last evaluation pass ending before the commit at 112,607; the next gather reloads mail at the new period. Successive closure and the two physical continuations found no needed-data loss. This is a schedule argument for normal execution, not a proof under every damaged state. The adaptation is disclosed in §25, but calling it literally “Gray's stage wipes” would be inaccurate and cannot transfer Gray's all-field spatial argument. Separately, §24 says `SelFrontEquivalenceTest` contains a changed-bit negative control. The test has random/forced equivalence assertions and **no mutant or changed-bit control**. Its six focused checks pass, and the sel-front gate numbers are correct (`focused_tests.log`, `sel_front_cost.log`). **Invalidates:** only those source-fidelity/test-method claims. **Fix:** describe the exact wipe schedule and add a real negative control to the sel-front test.
